The bond market quirks evident again. Power Finance Corporation (PFC) canceled
its debt issuance the third time on account of higher yield demanded by the
market. The benchmark 10-year is around ~6.4 percent and PFC's 2-year bond
issue, which got canceled, received bids at ~7.3 percent. Is this due to a
liquidity crunch or something specific to PFC?

Axis bank recently raised a large amount via a 10 year infrastructure bond for
about 7.27 percent in November, while PFC raised ₹3,000 crore on a competing
10-year bond at 7.08% on the same bidding day. However, its short-term issues
bids were coming uncomfortably high. Liquidity in the short end seems to be
highly squeezed.

The 25 bps cut in repo rate (to 5.25 percent) may not be sufficient. The falling
rupee due to dollar outflows and higher tariffs on Indian exports set up a
bleaker outlook for Indian currency and balance of payments situation.
Supporting the falling rupee is straining the liquidity situation further as the
RBI sells US dollars from its foreign exchange reserves into the currency market
and absorbs rupees.
