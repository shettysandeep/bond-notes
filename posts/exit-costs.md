One distinction between FDs and bonds that is overlooked often is the `price` of early exit - i.e. the first order costs of liquidating the bond or FD before its completes its term or maturity. In the case of FDs, this cost is known as an "early withdrawal penalty".

If you must liquidate a FD before its full term, you will be charged a penalty, which is not a rupee amount but a reduction in the interest rate paid on the FD. And it ranges from 0.5% to 1.0%.

An [example](https://www.policybazaar.com/fd-interest-rates/hdfc-bank-fd-rates/fd-premature-withdrawal-calculator/) of how this translates:

> You book a 2-year FD at 7.00%. You break it early at exactly 1 year. If the bank's 1-year FD rate on your booking date was 6.00%, and they charge a 1% penalty, your effective payout interest rate drops to 5.00% (6.00% - 1.00%)

In the case of bonds, the penalty is circumstantial. Setting aside the liquidity related impacts, exiting bonds early depends on the surrounding environment. If this is during a time when general interest rates are higher relative to the time you invested, you are likely to exit at a lower price than what you paid and thus incur a capital loss. This is due to the sensitivity of bond prices to interest rates and their inverse relationship. On the contrary, in a regime with softer interest rates (or expectation) you will likely receive a higher price on the bond and pocket a capital gain instead. There is also a liquidity cost if the bond you are tyring to offload has only few buyers.

Both instruments have an early withdrawal penalty but they present themselves differently. With FDs the penalty is fixed and mentioned in the fineprint. In the case of bonds, the cost of exit is hard to pinpoint in advance when it comes to bonds.
