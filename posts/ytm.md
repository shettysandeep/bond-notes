`Yield to maturity (YTM)` is often quoted for bonds - government as well as
corporate bonds - as a metric to summarize the return on these investments. YTM
is the internal rate of return (IRR) of the bond's cash flows. It is the rate
that equates the present value of all future cash flows to the current price of
the bond.

The YTM can be thought of as the return under two conditions:

1. The instruments are held until maturity.
2. The coupons received are reinvested at the same interest rate.

If either of these conditions are not satsiftied the actual returns deviate from the quoted YTM.

The second assumption is much more difficult to satisfy than the first. Finding an
alternative with the same interest rate to reinvest the coupon is not easy.
specially if one is in a low interest rate environment. This
is known as `reinvestment risk`.

"It is the chance that an investor will not be able to reinvest
cash flows—such as periodic coupon payments, dividend distributions, or matured
principal—at a rate of return equal to or higher than their current
investment."

Having said that it is possible that the coupon is invested in a higher yielding
instrument. In either case, the final return is markedly different from the
YTM quoted on the bonds.
